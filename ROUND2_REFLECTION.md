## 1. What was the most uncomfortable trade-off you made because of the time pressure?
The most uncomfortable trade-off was shipping pricing change notifications as a manually triggered backend flow instead of a fully automated, idempotent system. I added `GET /api/detect-changes` for inspection and `POST /api/detect-changes` for sending notifications, but I stopped short of building scheduled execution, delivery logs, and resend protection. That means the feature demonstrates the full product loop technically, yet it still depends on an external trigger and could send duplicate alerts if called repeatedly. I made that trade-off because the core value in this round was proving that the app could remember historical pricing context and tell users when their recommendation changed later. Building automation, scheduling, and notification history would have been the more polished version, but it also would have consumed time that I needed for the data model, diff logic, and testing. I chose "real but slightly manual" over "fully automated but incomplete."

## 2. If we extended the deadline by another 24 hours right now, what's the first thing you'd do?
The first thing I would do is add idempotent scheduled detection with notification history. Concretely, I would create a table that records which audit saw which pricing-change fingerprint and whether a notification was already sent for it. Then I would wire a scheduled job to call the detection flow automatically and only email users when a new fingerprint appears. That would turn the current implementation from a working demo into an operational feature I would be comfortable leaving alone in production. I would do this before improving templates or building a UI because it addresses the biggest product risk: accidental duplicate emails and reliance on a human to run the check. Once that is in place, the rest of the work becomes incremental polish instead of foundational reliability work.

## 3. Looking back at your Round 1 codebase as a now-experienced user of it: what's one thing your Round 1 self made harder for your Round 2 self?
My Round 1 self optimized too heavily for a one-shot shareable report flow and not enough for future statefulness. The app could calculate a good answer and render a report, but there was no durable concept of "this exact audit, with this exact pricing context, created at this exact moment." That made Round 2 harder because I had to retrofit persistence, introduce a stable `auditId`, preserve original user input, store rendered results, and snapshot pricing definitions without breaking the existing paths. None of that work was impossible, but it added friction at every layer because the original architecture assumed the audit was transient. If I were starting over, I would still keep the product simple, but I would establish a first-class stored audit model on day one even if the UI never exposed it initially. That single decision would have made this round much smoother.
